# Collateral and Valuation Policy (Illustrative)

Fictional document for prototype. Sets out acceptable collateral types, valuation standards, empanelled valuers, and monitoring requirements.

## 1. Acceptable Collateral Types

### COL-1.1 Primary collateral
Acceptable primary collateral types: (a) first-charge mortgage over commercial or industrial real estate; (b) first-charge hypothecation of plant and machinery; (c) pledge of listed securities (haircut applied per the Securities Haircut Schedule); (d) assignment of receivables under an approved receivables finance programme; (e) bank guarantees and standby letters of credit from investment-grade banks.

### COL-1.2 Secondary / supportive collateral
Personal guarantees from promoters, corporate guarantees from group companies with investment-grade ratings, and charge over intellectual property may be taken as secondary collateral. Secondary collateral does not count toward the LTV ratio calculation.

### COL-1.3 Prohibited collateral
The following may not be accepted as collateral: cryptocurrency or digital assets; private company shares unless listed on an approved exchange within 6 months; assets located in sanctions-designated jurisdictions; and assets subject to existing prior charges that the Bank has not consented to.

## 2. Valuation Standards

### COL-2.1 Independent valuation requirement
All primary collateral must be valued by an independent valuer who appears on the Bank's approved valuer panel. Valuations prepared by valuers connected to the borrower, RM, or Credit Analyst are not acceptable.

### COL-2.2 Valuation age
A valuation report must be dated no more than 180 days before the credit decision date. For volatile asset classes (listed securities, commodity stocks), the Bank uses mark-to-market pricing and no static valuation report is required.

### COL-2.3 Valuation methodology
Valuations for real estate must be prepared on a Market Value basis (RICS Red Book or equivalent). For plant and machinery, both Market Value and Depreciated Replacement Cost must be reported. The more conservative figure is used for LTV purposes.

### COL-2.4 Forced sale value
Where the credit memo flags elevated risk, the Credit Committee may instruct that the Forced Sale Value (FSV) be used for LTV rather than Market Value. FSV is defined as 75% of Market Value unless the valuer provides a specific FSV.

## 3. Approved Valuer Panel

### COL-3.1 Panel membership criteria
To join the approved valuer panel, a firm must: hold professional indemnity insurance of at least USD 5 million; employ at least one Fellow of the relevant national valuation institute; have no connected-party relationship with the Bank, its directors, or its major shareholders; and pass the Bank's annual due diligence review.

### COL-3.2 Current empanelled valuers
Panel members (illustrative): Apex Property Valuers LLP, Global Asset Assessors Inc., Meridian Real Estate Advisory, PrecisionVal Services, Sterling Collateral Consultants. The full and current panel list is maintained by the Credit Administration team and updated quarterly.

### COL-3.3 Panel suspension
A panelled valuer may be suspended immediately by the Head of Credit Risk if: a material error in a valuation report is identified; the firm is subject to regulatory investigation; or the firm's professional indemnity insurance lapses. Suspension is communicated to all RMs within 24 hours.

## 4. LTV Policy

### COL-4.1 Standard LTV limits
Maximum LTV: 70% for commercial real estate (first charge). 60% for plant and machinery. 50% for listed securities (after haircut). 65% for receivables (advance rate).

### COL-4.2 LTV monitoring
LTV is monitored at least annually, or immediately on a material adverse change in collateral value. Where LTV exceeds the policy maximum, the Bank invokes the additional-security covenant and the borrower must remedy within 30 days.

### COL-4.3 Top-up covenant
All secured term facilities include a covenant requiring the borrower to provide additional collateral or repay the excess if LTV at any monitoring date exceeds the policy maximum by more than 5 percentage points.

## 5. Insurance Requirements

### COL-5.1 Asset insurance
The borrower must maintain comprehensive insurance on all charged assets (fire, flood, damage, and business interruption where applicable) for their full replacement value. The Bank must be noted as loss payee.

### COL-5.2 Insurance monitoring
The Credit Administration team collects insurance renewal certificates annually. A lapse in insurance coverage triggers a covenant breach notice within 5 business days.
