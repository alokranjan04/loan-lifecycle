# KYC, AML and Customer Due Diligence Policy (Illustrative)

Fictional document for prototype. Sets out the Bank's obligations under anti-money-laundering legislation and internal risk appetite for customer acceptance.

## 1. Objectives

### KYC-1.1 Policy objective
The Bank must know who its customers are, understand the nature of their business, and assess the money-laundering and terrorist-financing risk they present. This policy operationalises those obligations and sets minimum standards for all business lines.

### KYC-1.2 Regulatory basis
This policy is aligned with FATF Recommendations 10–12, the Bank Secrecy Act (BSA), and the Bank's home jurisdiction AML Act. Where local regulations impose higher standards, the local standard prevails.

### KYC-1.3 Scope
Applies to all new customers, all existing customers at periodic review, and to any material change in ownership or business activity.

## 2. Customer Identification Programme

### KYC-2.1 Legal entity identification
For corporate borrowers, the Bank must obtain: certificate of incorporation, memorandum and articles of association, latest register of members, board resolution authorising the facility, and a valid trading licence where applicable.

### KYC-2.2 Individual identification
For each director and beneficial owner: a government-issued photo ID (passport or national ID card) valid on the date of the credit decision, and one proof of address dated within the last 3 months.

### KYC-2.3 Beneficial ownership threshold
Any natural person holding 25% or more of the voting rights or economic interest in the borrower entity is a beneficial owner and must be identified and verified. Ownership chains must be traced to the ultimate beneficial owner (UBO).

### KYC-2.4 Document expiry
Expired identification documents must be refreshed before disbursement of funds. A 14-day grace period applies only where the document expired after the credit decision date and renewal is in progress.

## 3. Risk Classification

### KYC-3.1 Standard due diligence
Applied to customers with low or medium AML risk: domestic companies with transparent ownership, established operating history, and revenues from identifiable business activities.

### KYC-3.2 Enhanced due diligence trigger
EDD is mandatory when: (a) the customer or any UBO is a PEP or close associate of a PEP; (b) the customer operates in a FATF high-risk jurisdiction; (c) the customer's business model involves high-cash activity; (d) the customer is a virtual asset service provider; or (e) the RM or screening system flags unusual patterns.

### KYC-3.3 Risk scoring model
The Bank's AML risk model assigns a composite score (Low / Medium / High) using: jurisdiction risk, industry risk, ownership complexity, customer size, and adverse media history. The score is recorded on the customer profile and drives the review frequency.

## 4. Ongoing Monitoring

### KYC-4.1 Periodic review cycle
Low-risk customers: reviewed every 3 years. Medium-risk: annually. High-risk (including PEPs): every 6 months. The system generates review tasks automatically based on the last review date.

### KYC-4.2 Trigger reviews
A review is triggered immediately on: change of beneficial ownership, adverse news alert, unusual transaction pattern, or a request from compliance or law enforcement.

### KYC-4.3 Transaction monitoring
All account activity is monitored against behaviour baselines and AML typology rules. Alerts generated by the transaction monitoring system must be resolved by the AML analyst within 10 business days.

## 5. Suspicious Activity Reporting

### KYC-5.1 SAR obligation
Any employee who knows or reasonably suspects that a customer is engaged in money laundering or terrorist financing must report this to the MLRO immediately. Tipping off the customer is prohibited.

### KYC-5.2 MLRO decision timeline
The MLRO must assess each referral and decide within 7 business days whether to file a Suspicious Activity Report (SAR) with the financial intelligence unit. The decision and rationale are documented in the case management system.
