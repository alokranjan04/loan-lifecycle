# Risk Appetite Statement and Concentration Limits (Illustrative)

Fictional document for prototype. Defines the Board's risk appetite for commercial credit and the portfolio concentration limits that operationalise it.

## 1. Risk Appetite Statement

### RAS-1.1 Board statement
The Board accepts moderate credit risk in pursuit of sustainable commercial lending returns. The Bank targets a net credit loss rate of below 0.8% of average gross loans in a normal economic environment, and below 2.0% in a severe but plausible stress scenario.

### RAS-1.2 Appetite by grade
The Bank's portfolio should be composed of: Grades 1–4: minimum 60% of total commercial credit exposure. Grades 5–6: maximum 35%. Grades 7–10: maximum 5%. Exposure in Grades 8–10 triggers mandatory remediation plans.

### RAS-1.3 Non-performing loan ratio
The Board's risk appetite for the non-performing loan (NPL) ratio (gross NPL / gross loans) is below 3.5%. Above 4.0% triggers a Board-level remediation review.

## 2. Sector Appetite

### RAS-2.1 Grow sectors
Sectors where the Bank actively seeks to increase exposure: agri and food processing; healthcare and pharmaceuticals; renewable energy; affordable housing construction; technology and IT services; logistics and warehousing.

### RAS-2.2 Maintain sectors
Sectors where the Bank accepts new business to maintain current share: commercial real estate; general manufacturing; retail and wholesale trade; education and training; professional services.

### RAS-2.3 Reduce sectors
Sectors where the Bank accepts renewals but actively reduces share: hospitality and tourism (post-pandemic recovery uncertain); conventional power generation; automotive retail.

### RAS-2.4 Exit sectors
Sectors where no new facilities are approved: coal mining; thermal coal power generation; tobacco manufacturing; fur and exotic leather production; unlicensed payday lending. Existing facilities in Exit sectors run to maturity and are not renewed.

## 3. Concentration Limits

### RAS-3.1 Single obligor limit
Total exposure to a single obligor group (all facilities, on- and off-balance-sheet) must not exceed USD 25 million or 10% of Tier 1 capital, whichever is lower.

### RAS-3.2 Sector concentration limit
Total exposure to any single sector (using the Bank's 2-digit sector classification) must not exceed 25% of total commercial credit book.

### RAS-3.3 Geography concentration limit
Total exposure to any single country (for cross-border lending) must not exceed 15% of total commercial credit book. Exposure to FATF high-risk jurisdictions is capped at 2%.

### RAS-3.4 Connected-party limit
Total exposure to all connected parties (directors, major shareholders, and their associates) must not exceed 5% of Tier 1 capital. Any connected-party transaction above USD 500,000 requires Board Credit Committee approval.

## 4. Stress Testing

### RAS-4.1 Annual stress test
The Credit Risk team conducts an annual portfolio stress test using a severe-but-plausible macroeconomic scenario approved by the Board. Results are reported to the Board Risk Committee and used to calibrate the risk appetite thresholds.

### RAS-4.2 Ad hoc stress tests
An ad hoc stress test is run within 10 business days of: a rating agency placing the Bank on negative watch; a significant adverse market event; or a regulatory directive. The CCO initiates and the results are reported to the Board.

### RAS-4.3 Concentration breach response
When any concentration limit is breached, the Head of Credit Risk notifies the Board Risk Committee within 5 business days. A remediation plan (reducing exposure or increasing capital allocation) is agreed within 30 days.

## 5. Monitoring and Reporting

### RAS-5.1 Monthly portfolio report
The Credit Risk team publishes a monthly portfolio quality report covering: grade distribution, sector and obligor concentration, NPL ratio, watch-list movements, and compliance with concentration limits.

### RAS-5.2 Board reporting
The Board Risk Committee receives a quarterly credit risk dashboard and an annual comprehensive review. Any breach of a Board-level appetite threshold is reported at the next scheduled meeting, or ad hoc if the breach is material.
